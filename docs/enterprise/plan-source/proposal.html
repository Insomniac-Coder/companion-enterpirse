Companion Enterprise

One AI assistant for the whole company, on our terms
6 October 2026 Working title Builds on the Local LLM PC Companion

01The goal

One AI assistant that everyone in the company can use for everyday work: asking questions, getting answers from our own documents with the sources named (RAG), writing and reviewing code, and working with Jira and Outlook. It runs on AI models we host ourselves, or on approved outside providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, always under the company's rules and with a record of how it is used.

02Where it comes from: the Local LLM PC Companion

The Local LLM PC Companion is a working proof of concept built in-house. It runs open-weight AI models entirely on a laptop. It offers chat, answers questions about code, and has an agent that reads and writes project files, runs commands and checks its own work. Permission modes decide what the agent may do without asking, memory is managed automatically on long tasks, and everything is logged.

It proves that the core works, on modest hardware. What it lacks is what a company needs around it: today it serves one person, runs one model at a time, and lives on one PC.

03What the enterprise version adds

Company sign-in. People use their Microsoft 365 account; teams and roles come from the company directory.
Many people, many models. Several models served to everyone at once; servers are added or removed as demand changes.
Our models or outside providers. Open-weight models on our own hardware, or OpenAI, Anthropic and Google through one gateway, behind the same rules.
Answers from our documents (RAG). The assistant first finds the relevant passages in company, team or personal document collections, then answers from them and names its sources; each person only gets passages from documents they are allowed to open.
Jira and Outlook. Each person connects their own account; sending an email or changing a ticket always asks first.
Tools that IT controls (MCP). Plug-in tools are MCP servers, the open standard for connecting AI to other systems; IT adds or removes them and gives them to the teams that need them, and the system checks that a model can actually use a tool before offering it.
Guards and an audit log. Rules on what may leave the company, approval before actions, spending limits, and a tamper-evident record of who asked what.
A desktop app and an admin dashboard. An installable app for everyone, and a separate dashboard for the team that runs the service.

06The plan

FoundationsCompany sign-in, users and teams, a shared database, several models at once, outside providers.
Everyday useThe desktop app, plug-in tools (MCP), Jira and Outlook.
PilotRAG: answers from that department's documents. One department, 20 to 30 people, on one server.
Company-readyGuards and audit complete, many servers with automatic scaling, a security review.

Each step ends with a review before the next one starts. Where we are (6 October 2026): the Companion has been copied as the base with all its existing checks passing, and every part of its code has been mapped onto this design. Time estimates follow from that map.

07What we need from the company

  • A Microsoft Entra ID app registration, for sign-in and Outlook
  • Accounts with the outside providers we want to offer
  • One GPU server (or the AWS equivalent) and a pilot department
  • A code-signing certificate for the desktop app
  • A decision on hosting: our own servers or AWS
